Apparatus dew point

BF = (t_leave − ADP) / (t_enter − ADP)  ⇔  ADP = (t_leave − BF·t_enter) / (1 − BF)
  • From bypass factor: pick BF from the table for the application (or from the coil selection), then solve for ADP from the dry-bulb temperatures.
  • From chart: the straight line from entering state E through leaving state L is extended to the saturation curve; the crossing is the ADP. BF then follows from the equation above. This uses both dry and wet bulb, so it also checks whether the leaving state is achievable.
  • Physically the ADP cannot be above the leaving air dew point, because leaving air is a mix of bypassed entering air and saturated air at the ADP.

Chilled water temperature entering the coil

t_w = 2·t_ad − t_wb
  • t_w: coil entering water temperature; t_ad: apparatus dew point; t_wb: coil leaving air wet-bulb temperature.
  • Source: ASHRAE Handbook—HVAC Systems and Equipment (2016, SI), Chapter 13 “Hydronic Heating and Cooling”, Eq. (11).
  • The same chapter notes that, for a space held at 24 °C / 50% RH (dew point about 13 °C), return water should be near 13 °C (16 °C at most), and that about 2 °C is the lowest practical chilled water temperature. Chiller limits (refrigerant, minimum flow) must be confirmed with the manufacturer.
