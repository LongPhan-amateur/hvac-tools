How the air is followed through the AHU

The calculation is a steady design-cooling case. Each section changes the dry-bulb temperature t and the humidity ratio W of the dry-air mass flow ṁ passing through it; enthalpy is h = 1.006·t + W·(2501 + 1.86·t). Moist-air properties follow ASHRAE Handbook – Fundamentals 2021, Ch. 1 Psychrometrics: saturation pressure Eq. (5)/(6), humidity ratio Eq. (20), specific volume Eq. (26), enthalpy Eq. (30), wet bulb Eq. (33)/(35), barometric pressure from altitude Eq. (3). Air mass flow is V̇ / v at the actual state, so no standard-air factor (1.23 / 3010) is needed and altitude is included.

  • Outdoor and return air: outdoor (or MAU leaving) state from DB/WB; return air = room state plus the return temperature rise and the return-fan heat.
  • Energy recovery (outdoor air ↔ exhaust): Q = ε·Cmin·(tOA − tEA); the wheel also transfers moisture with the latent effectiveness. If recovery would add heat or moisture to the outdoor air, the device is taken as bypassed.
  • Mixing: mass-weighted W and h of outdoor and return air. Return air reaching the AHU = supply − room leakage; relief / exhaust = return − recirculated air. The mixing box can be with or without OA/RA dampers.
  • Filters: the pre-filter (one or two stages) is on the mixed air just before the final filter; the final filter is before the coils or on the discharge after the supply fan. With a heat recovery wheel, the exhaust side gets a 1- or 2-stage filter (top deck) and an optional outdoor-air filter protects the supply side of the wheel. A desiccant wheel has its own reactivation deck below the supply deck: outdoor air → reactivation fan → 1- or 2-stage filter → heater → wheel → exhaust (fan power = reactivation airflow × fan pressure ÷ efficiency) (reactivation airflow = process airflow ÷ the process : reactivation ratio); it is kept separate from the return / exhaust deck that serves the heat recovery wheel.
  • Desiccant wheel (simplified): dries the air to the set dew point; the process air heats by about 1.1 × the latent heat removed; reactivation heat ≈ factor × latent heat removed.
  • Wrap-around heat pipe / run-around coil: pre-cools by ε·(tin − tcoil leaving) (not below the air dew point) and gives the same heat back after the cooling coil.
  • Cooling coil (chilled water or DX): leaves at the set DB and dew point (or keeps W when the entering air is already drier). Qtotal = ṁ·[(hin − hout) − (Win − Wout)·hw], hw = 4.186·tout (ASHRAE Fundamentals 2021, Ch. 1 Eq. (43) and (32)); condensate = ṁ·ΔW. The apparatus dew point is where the line through the coil entering and leaving states meets saturation, and the required chilled water supply is tw = 2·tad − twb,leaving (ASHRAE HVAC Systems & Equipment 2016, Ch. 13, Eq. 11). Water flow = Q / (4.186·ΔT). For a DX coil the check is SST ≤ ADP − minimum approach (default 3 K); the condensing unit input is estimated as Q / EER.
  • Reheat: heats to the supply target, less the draw-through fan temperature rise. Hot-water flow = Q / (4.186·ΔT).
  • Humidifier: raises W to the minimum supply dew point (steam: constant DB; adiabatic: constant enthalpy).
  • Fans: total pressure = external static + sum of component pressure drops; absorbed power = V̇·Δp / ηtotal. All absorbed power is taken as heat into the airstream (motor in air stream): Δt = P / (ṁ·cp). Motor per fan = next standard size ≥ absorbed power per fan × (1 + margin).
  • Pre-cooling coil: optional first coil in series, chilled water (e.g. 11/17 °C) or DX cooling to a set leaving DB; when wet it leaves at about 95 % RH. Its load uses Eq. (43) and its water check tw = 2·ADP − twb like the main coil (e.g. 7/12 °C); for DX the check is SST ≤ ADP − approach.
  • Discharge order: supply fan → UV-C lamp → final filter (when after the fan) → sound attenuator → discharge plenum.
  • Height & decks: leave the casing height blank for an automatic size (width ≈ 1.5 × height), or set it: the width then follows from the face velocity. The height is split between the supply deck, the return / exhaust deck (return fan, energy recovery) and the desiccant reactivation deck in proportion to their face areas (supply at the set face velocity, return at 3 m/s, reactivation at 2.5 m/s).
  • Process report: the Calculation tab shows a process schematic with every state point; The return line runs from the room through the return fan to the mixing box and, for the exhaust part, through the exhaust side of the heat recovery to outside. Airflows are the design airflow of each stream in m³/h (the dry-air mass flow is constant along a stream; the actual volume changes slightly with density). “Export process report (PDF)” prints the state point data (air flow, DB, WB, RH, W, v, h, dew point, density, vapour pressure, absolute humidity) and each process (mixing, coils, fan heat, recovery) with total, sensible and latent energy, moisture change, SHR and Δh/ΔW.
  • Size and weight (estimate): face area = supply airflow / coil face velocity, width ≈ 1.5 × height, plus panels; length is the sum of the section lengths (inlet and discharge plenums, mixing box, every component and each access / maintenance section). A length or weight entered in a component card replaces the estimate. Operating weight sums 50 mm double-skin panels, base frame, and typical weights per m² of face area for each section (wet coils included). Use the manufacturer's selection for the final figures.

Linking with the other tools

All tools on this site share the browser's storage. “Link data” reads the room summaries of the dew point & supply air tool (airflow, room condition, coil leaving DB and dew point, supply temperature) and, when present, the chilled water tool (required chilled water supply and ΔT). Rooms are grouped by AC equipment name into AHUs: airflows are added, and the coil and supply targets follow the most demanding room (lowest dew point / coil leaving temperature).
