Equations

Moist air properties follow the ASHRAE Handbook – Fundamentals 2021 (SI), Ch. 1 Psychrometrics: saturation pressure Eq. (5)/(6) (Hyland–Wexler), humidity ratio Eq. (20), specific volume Eq. (26), enthalpy Eq. (30), wet bulb Eq. (33)/(35), pressure from altitude Eq. (3); dew point by inverting Eq. (5)/(6) with Eq. (36).

p = 101.325 × (1 − 2.25577·10⁻⁵ × Z)^5.2559  [kPa] W = 0.621945 × p_w / (p − p_w)  [kg/kg dry air] h = 1.006·t + W·(2501 + 1.86·t)  [kJ/kg] v = 0.287042·(t + 273.15)·(1 + 1.607858·W) / p  [m³/kg]

The dew point is the temperature at which the saturation vapor pressure equals the partial vapor pressure p_w. The tool solves it by bisection.

Supply air temperature selection

Standard air: Q_sens = 1.23 · Q · ΔT  [kW, m³/s, K] → ṁ = 1.23 · Q / c_p  (ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, c_p ≈ 1.025; ASHRAE Fundamentals Ch. 18) Actual: ṁ = Q / v  (v at the supply or room state, Eq. 26) T_s = T_r − Q_sens / (ṁ·c_p) ;   c_p = 1.006 + 1.86·W_r

The coil, reheat and supply states depend only on the mass flow ṁ. With the ΔT method ṁ = Q_sens / (c_p·ΔT), so the same sensible load and ΔT always give the same mass flow. On standard air the volume flow is Q = Q_sens / (1.23·ΔT), identical for every room state. On an actual basis it changes slightly with the specific volume and the moist-air c_p (e.g. supply 17 °C vs 13 °C differ by about 1.4 %). Use standard air for comparing cases and schedules, and the actual basis (usually at the fan, i.e. the supply state) when the altitude is high or the fan is selected at actual conditions.

W_s = W_r − ṁ_w / ṁ ;   ṁ_w = Q_lat / 2501  (or kg/h ÷ 3600)

Sensible heat ratio line & ADP

RSHR = Q_sens / (Q_sens + Q_lat) ;   Q_lat = ṁ_w × 2501  [kW] dW/dt along the line = c_p·(1 − RSHR) / (2501·RSHR) = c_p·ṁ_w / Q_sens ADP: W_r − (T_r − t)·dW/dt = W_sat(t) ;   BF = (T_s − ADP) / (T_r − ADP)
  • The RSHR line passes through the room point. Every supply state on it removes sensible and latent load in the right proportion, so the room holds design conditions. The supply point S always lies on this line.
  • Where the line meets the saturation curve is the apparatus dew point (ADP), the lowest effective coil surface temperature. If the line never reaches saturation, a coil alone cannot hold the room humidity.
  • BF is the equivalent coil bypass factor if the coil cooled room air directly to T_s (no outdoor air, no reheat).
  • The coil leaving temperature needed for dehumidification is the temperature at which air with W = W_s reaches the “coil leaving RH” (default 95%).
  • If that temperature is below T_s: the latent load governs. The coil must cool deeper, then the air is reheated to T_s.
  • If it is at or above T_s: the sensible load governs. The coil cools to T_s with no reheat, and the room may run drier than design.
  • A chilled water coil can only bring air down to about (CHWS + approach). Below that you need colder chilled water or a desiccant dehumidifier.
  • Diffusers and supply ducts have a surface temperature close to T_s, so T_s must be above the dew point of the surrounding air plus the safety margin. Otherwise insulate, or choose suitable diffusers.

Cases, night case and recommendation

Each room can be calculated for several cases (1, 2, …): for example different airflow, ΔT or coil leaving RH. For every case the night / low-load condition is checked: the moisture load stays, the room sensible load drops to the minimum entered, so the coil must still dry the air to the same dew point while the supply has to be warmer, Ts,n = troom − qs,min / (ṁ·cp). Reheat at night = ṁ·cp·(Ts,n − Tcoil); the reheat to select is the larger of the design and night values. Without it the room RH rises (estimate shown in the check).

Set point band: when the cases of a room cover a range of room conditions and one case sits at the centre of that range (the set point, e.g. 21 °C / 50 % inside 20–22 °C / 40–60 % from “Auto generate cases”), that case is the design case and is recommended unless it fails; the corner cases are only checks, and the reason lists which corners the design holds and which it does not.

Otherwise (cases are alternative designs for the same room) the recommendation per room: cases with a failed check are excluded; then the case with the fewest notes (condensation margins, duct insulation, low dew point, large ΔT); if still equal, the larger coil + reheat load is chosen as a safety margin (coil load on room air, reheat = larger of design and night); then the larger airflow. The AHU and chilled water tools link the recommended case.

Limitations

  • Outdoor air, mixing and coil load are not included. This tool only looks at the room heat and moisture balance.
  • Fan heat and duct heat gain are ignored. For detailed design, add roughly 0.5–1.5 K to the supply air temperature.
  • Results are for quick checks and preliminary selection. Final design should be verified with dedicated load calculation software.
