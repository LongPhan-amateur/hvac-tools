Equations

Moist air properties follow the ASHRAE Handbook – Fundamentals (Psychrometrics chapter), with saturation vapor pressure from Hyland–Wexler.

p = 101.325 × (1 − 2.25577·10⁻⁵ × Z)^5.2559  [kPa] W = 0.621945 × p_w / (p − p_w)  [kg/kg dry air] h = 1.006·t + W·(2501 + 1.86·t)  [kJ/kg] v = 0.287042·(t + 273.15)·(1 + 1.607858·W) / p  [m³/kg]

The dew point is the temperature at which the saturation vapor pressure equals the partial vapor pressure p_w. The tool solves it by bisection.

Supply air temperature selection

ṁ = Q / v_s ;   T_s = T_r − Q_sens / (ṁ·c_p) ;   c_p = 1.006 + 1.86·W_r W_s = W_r − ṁ_w / ṁ ;   ṁ_w = Q_lat / 2501  (or kg/h ÷ 3600)

Sensible heat ratio line & ADP

RSHR = Q_sens / (Q_sens + Q_lat) ;   Q_lat = ṁ_w × 2501  [kW] dW/dt along the line = c_p·(1 − RSHR) / (2501·RSHR) = c_p·ṁ_w / Q_sens ADP: W_r − (T_r − t)·dW/dt = W_sat(t) ;   BF = (T_s − ADP) / (T_r − ADP)
  • The RSHR line passes through the room point. Every supply state on it removes sensible and latent load in the right proportion, so the room holds design conditions. The supply point S always lies on this line.
  • Where the line meets the saturation curve is the apparatus dew point (ADP), the lowest effective coil surface temperature. If the line never reaches saturation, a coil alone cannot hold the room humidity.
  • BF is the equivalent coil bypass factor if the coil cooled room air directly to T_s (no outdoor air, no reheat).
  • The coil leaving temperature needed for dehumidification is the temperature at which air with W = W_s reaches the “coil leaving RH” (default 95%).
  • If that temperature is below T_s: the latent load governs. The coil must cool deeper, then the air is reheated to T_s.
  • If it is at or above T_s: the sensible load governs. The coil cools to T_s with no reheat, and the room may run drier than design.
  • A chilled water coil can only bring air down to about (CHWS + approach). Below that you need colder chilled water or a desiccant dehumidifier.
  • Diffusers and supply ducts have a surface temperature close to T_s, so T_s must be above the dew point of the surrounding air plus the safety margin. Otherwise insulate, or choose suitable diffusers.

Limitations

  • Outdoor air, mixing and coil load are not included. This tool only looks at the room heat and moisture balance.
  • Fan heat and duct heat gain are ignored. For detailed design, add roughly 0.5–1.5 K to the supply air temperature.
  • Results are for quick checks and preliminary selection. Final design should be verified with dedicated load calculation software.
